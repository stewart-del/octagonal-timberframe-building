# Open timber-framed porch proposal

The open covered porch is centered on the existing P2–P3 west doorway and follows that wall's actual model axes. Its clear floor measures 8 × 8 feet. Posts carry a small gabled roof; open side and front railings surround a centered passage to the exterior stairs. The porch starts six inches below the existing main-room floor, creating one step up into the octagon. The threshold cap meets the actual bamboo floor edge and its underside bears on the existing subfloor.

The washdown floor falls ¼ inch per foot away from the doorway. A four-inch-wide linear grate at the outside edge is approximately eight inches below the main floor. Ninety-six bars have real half-inch clear openings, with two edge rails resting on channel ledges. The channel falls sideways toward a three-inch-square open outlet. The walking grate has a clear upward removal path for cleaning. Waterproofing, drain flanges, trap, cleanout, discharge destination and freeze protection remain design work; the model does not discharge wastewater onto grade.

Two level benches, each eighteen inches deep, leave a five-foot center aisle. Side railings directly behind them provide back support. Their top rails sit twenty inches above the seats, 38–40 inches above the sloping floor; the front railing is 36 inches above the front floor. Pickets continue below the seats, with clear gaps no greater than 3½ inches. Bench legs meet the sloped floor, shoe shelves fit below the seats, and six coat hooks sit on boards beside the existing doorway.

The gable runs outward from the octagon at a 4:12 pitch. Its cedar surface reaches model Y = 2.725 m at the ridge. The roof uses the same cedar-shingle appearance as the main building, above a twenty-millimeter deck. Its rear covering is fitted to the main roof's upper surface, with modeled flashing. All new rafters begin outside the original roof's eave; the addition does not cut the original frame.

Five rafter pairs use proposed actual 3½ × 5½-inch stock. Their apexes have pegged half-laps and their feet have horizontal birdsmouth seats with full 3½ × 5½-inch bearing. Level 5½-inch-square side beams carry the feet. Four transverse ties enter open-top half-laps in those beams. The roof covering touches every rafter. The horizontal front header joins the side beams; all four corner posts have shouldered top tenons. Rafter uplift restraint, roof loads, ties, pegs and spans still require a project-specific structural design.

The model contains six knee braces cut from full actual 2 × 6-inch stock: four in the side frames and two across the front. Each has an outer-face housing and fitted peg, with a single normal-to-frame insertion direction that works at both ends. This follows the face-cut alternative described by [Vermont Timber Works](https://www.vermonttimberworks.com/ask-the-timber-framing-experts/can-a-mortise-tenon-brace-be-installed-after-the-post-beam-have-been-assembled/). Their explanation also identifies why a conventional centered brace cannot simply be pushed into two closed, fixed corner mortises. The reference establishes the joint family; the proposed porch dimensions and peg sizes are not copied from an approved design.

The 3½-inch-deep guard rails have ¼-inch housings and 1½ × 1½-inch tenons extending another inch into their posts. Perpendicular joints are staggered vertically. Pickets have matching half-inch-square tenons at both ends. Post heads, feet, front header and guard rails use actual mating mortises and tenons, secured where shown by separate ⅜- or ½-inch wood pegs. These are traditional joint families, consistent with the [Timber Framers Guild's description of pegged mortise-and-tenon construction and assembled bents](https://www.tfguild.org/timber-framing-the-basics). Remaining wood, peg edge distances, shrinkage and connection capacities need timber-framer and engineering review.

The nominal assembly sequence is:

1. Join each side's top guard rail between movable posts. Seat its pickets in the base rail, then lower the post/top-rail subassembly onto the picket and post-foot tenons.
2. Preassemble the front guard panels and entry posts. Bring the side bents together around the transverse front panels before fixing both bents in position.
3. Assemble the two side roof beams and front header as a U, then lower that assembly onto the four post-head tenons.
4. Lower the transverse ties into their open-top seats. Insert the face-housed knee braces normal to each frame plane, then install the pegs.
5. Assemble and peg each rafter pair at its apex, lower it onto the birdsmouth seats, and install the deck, cedar and flashing.

The continuous motion tests prove the listed local insertions, both receivers of each brace together, and the paired-rafter drop. They do not constitute a complete raising plan: temporary bracing, lifting, fixing order, access and construction tolerances still require planning. The model uses exact nominal mating geometry; real timber fabrication needs suitable tolerances and movement allowances.

A three-foot landing connects the open passage to six equal risers of approximately 6.46 inches, with five twelve-inch treads and a lower landing. Guards and sloped handrails are shown. Four supports meet the pan underside; stairs and landings are supported concrete masses. Reference grade, soils, below-grade foundations, reinforcement, anchors, accessibility and code compliance remain unresolved. Porch loads are separate from the original primary-timber inventory and self-weight calculation.
