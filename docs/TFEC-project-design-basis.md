# Octagon website — TFEC design basis and release gates

## Standing owner direction

Use the Timber Frame Engineering standard as the project's primary technical reference. This direction establishes a design discipline, not certification of the present model.

## Verified edition and authority

Checked **2026-11-02**: the [official TFEC standards catalogue](https://www.tfguild.org/timber-frame-engineering-council/standards) lists **TFEC 1-2019, Standard for Design of Timber Frame Structures and Commentary**, published January 15, 2019. No later TFEC 1 edition is listed there. This is the latest edition verified from the publisher, not a claim that every jurisdiction adopts it. [Official document](https://www.tfguild.org/timber-frame-engineering-council/standards/view/104/download).

TFEC §1.1 places it alongside NDS, with NDS prevailing in conflicts. §§1.2 and 1.4 require professional interpretation and applicable design loads. The project's jurisdiction, adopted code/NDS edition and engineer of record remain unconfirmed. Do not automatically substitute today's newest code edition for the legally applicable one.

The catalogue also lists TFEC 2-2018 (practice) and TFEC 3-2019 (existing-structure evaluation); they are not newer editions of TFEC 1. A physically existing example does not establish the reconstructed model's joints, materials or capacity.

## Website and repository protocol

1. Keep a visible “TFEC-informed design review — not structurally qualified” status and official reference link. Never show “TFEC compliant,” “engineered,” “safe to build,” or an equivalent approval without documented professional verification for the exact revision.
2. Each joint family gets a review record: geometry revision, intended force directions, contact/retention assumptions, section references, evidence, open questions and reviewer disposition. Use “not checked,” “input missing,” “calculated,” and “professionally reviewed” distinctly.
3. Red arrows remain **applied member self-weight**, with density scenario and scaling explained. They are not solved reactions, bearing pressure or strain. Warning symbols identify concerns, not proven failures; unmarked joints are not certified safe.
4. Pressure examples remain conditional R/A calculations. Actual pressure requires a solved normal reaction and justified engaged area. No numerical strain without the appropriate material model and solved stress/deformation state.
5. Any joint edit requires updated geometry, net volume, centroids, contact areas, anchors and restraint classification. Re-run collision/fit and regression tests; keep previous results tied to their old revision. Geometric tests cannot replace structural checks.
6. Design acceptance requires complete loads and material inputs, a stable global load path, member and connection checks, serviceability and erection review, and qualified structural review. Fabrication output remains preliminary until those gates are satisfied.
7. Cite section identifiers and concise original notes. Link the publisher's standard; do not reproduce its tables, commentary or full text in the site/repository.

No geometry, joint dimensions or structural certification were changed by this document.
