R02-S1 · MODEL AUDIT

Fit is checked.
Strength needs a complete connection design.

The current joints are not yet suitable as a fabrication or hurricane-resistance specification. The central withdrawal restraint, receiver proportions and continuous load path need redesign.

A simple dovetail is a candidate—not a universal upgrade

An integral flare can resist withdrawal along the member. The receiving cheeks must resist the spreading force, and the neck must carry tension. An open-entry dovetail can still move back out along its assembly direction. The diagram below describes that distinction; it supplies no cut dimensions.

Design direction: broad housed bearing for gravity; a plain dovetail only where its direction and assembly make sense; engineered pegs or concealed restraint where load reversal requires it. No ornamental carving and no driven wedge are proposed. A locked geometry still requires a capacity calculation.

Gravity and wind must be followed through every joint

Roof covering/deck → short rafters and hips → perimeter bearing and central cupola assembly → ring/tie system and posts → anchors → foundations. The cupola ceiling has a separate path through its spokes and upper frame. Lateral loads need the enclosed walls or a designed bracing/diaphragm system. The central opening must remain clear while the surrounding system transfers both compression and tension.

Main-roof horizontal projected area: 747.65 ft²; cupola-roof area: 61.60 ft². Each uniform 1 psf of vertical load therefore contributes 809.24 lb in total. A symmetric bookkeeping share is 93.46 + 7.70 ft² per radial bay. These are load coefficients, not joint reactions; asymmetric snow and local wind suction require separate cases.

CRITICAL · T0–T7 / R0–R7 / C0–C7 / cupola rings

Center has no designed tension and uplift restraint

The straight two-inch tongues have no pegs, bolts, retained dovetail or other positive withdrawal restraint. Nominal contact does not close the central force path.

Design response: Design the open-center drum/ring and its joints as one system. Evaluate a plain housed dovetail where radial withdrawal governs, with separate restraint against its insertion/removal direction. Do not assume an open-bottom polygon or plywood alone supplies the required ring action.

HIGH · R0–R7 to C0–C7

Main-rafter tongue exceeds the receiver-based proportion

The generic width rule uses the 8-inch rafter: 8/3 = 2.667 inches. In a 6-inch receiving post, nominal cheeks are only (6 - 2.667)/2 = 1.667 inches before neighboring cuts. TFEC 1-2019 §§3.4.10–11 use the receiving member and require each cheek at least the mortise width for the prescribed tension-joint provisions.

Design response: Redesign this joint from the receiving post and load demand. A broader head is not a remedy for thin cheeks. A dovetail is a different connection requiring its own neck, bearing and splitting/block-shear checks.

HIGH · All generic two-inch shoulder tenons

Two-inch insertion is not a ready-made pegged tension joint

No peg location or end distance is modeled. For illustration, the TFEC full geometry factor calls for 4D loaded-end distance in a softwood tension member: a 3/4-inch peg alone needs 3 inches from peg center to the loaded end. The current two-inch tongue cannot provide that distance.

Design response: Resolve species, peg diameter, receiving-face edge distances and joint arrangement together; do not simply drill the existing tongues. This comparison does not assign a capacity or prohibit every alternative tested detail.

HIGH · R/P heels and J/B seats

Outer seats carry gravity but do not lock against uplift

R0 bears across the 8 × 8 post head (64 in² nominal horizontal footprint). Each short rafter has approximately 12 in² of horizontal bearing on its ring beam. These are real support surfaces, not merely the colored cavity edge seen in the screenshot. No rated uplift or sliding restraint is modeled.

Design response: Keep broad gravity bearing; check heel/block shear, remaining rafter section and the signed force direction. Retain or adjust the seat only after those checks. Add a designed restraint for force reversal; a deeper notch alone is not a solution.

HIGH · K/B/P families and foundations

Braces, eave-ring joints and post bases need a complete lateral system

Braces use full-section diagonal sockets without a complete peg/housing design. Eave-ring joints share post heads with other cuts. No foundation anchorage is modeled. Enclosed walls are confirmed, but their sheathing and attachments are not.

Design response: Trace wall/roof shear through collectors, braces or shear panels, post joints and foundations. Check both wind directions, load reversal, combined notches and erection stability.

HIGH · CC/CR/PLY families and upper rings

Cupola ceiling and roof require attachments

The horizontal half-inch plywood and eight exposed horizontal ceiling spokes now follow the user-confirmed arrangement. Ceiling spoke 4 × 6 sizes and roof-rafter 2 × 6 depths are assumptions. Center miters touch but do not themselves resist separation. Finish boards are not assigned an invented thickness.

Design response: Design center and perimeter connections plus plywood grade, panel joints, edge blocking and fastening. Do not credit the plywood as a diaphragm until its complete boundary load path is specified.

REVIEW · Historical keyed version

Earlier prototype is not verified stronger

The earlier model used algorithmic keys and larger-looking interlocks. Its own engineering review recorded severely reduced webs and unresolved raising/tool access. Those shapes were not a verified traditional joinery schedule.

Design response: Retain useful viewing features and compare real housings/shoulders, but do not restore arbitrary keys as a structural fix.

REVIEW · All sockets

Geometric audit has a defined limit

Positive-volume pair overlaps, registered contact and connected meshes pass. The separate unfilled-socket volume diagnostics are nonzero and numerically inconsistent under rotation; complete cavity closure is not certified.

Design response: Resolve socket-volume closure independently before any fabrication claim. The contact-area proxy in the fit report is a geometry diagnostic, not a structural bearing-capacity calculation.

Inputs still needed

The building is confirmed enclosed. Actual location, species/grade/moisture, assembly weights, openings and foundation details remain unresolved. A timber-frame structural engineer needs those inputs to size the final joints and verify gravity, uplift, lateral and erection cases.
