1 · What was checked

The review uses the actual final fitted solids: 104 timbers, 1,789 convex cells and the existing part IDs. The source geometry remains unchanged. A fresh assembled fitted-solid overlap check reports zero member-pair intersections above 1e-10 m³, using convex-cell intersections with 1e-9 m distance and 1e-12 m³ cell-volume numerical thresholds. Bounding boxes only prune candidates. This does not approve bearing, cuts, retained wood, contact forces, stability, tool access or raising loads; the original source prisms and exploded poses are outside that check’s scope.

The fitted joinery was generated by dividing overlapping stock and transferring conceptual keys sized at 40% of nominal width and depth. Those were geometric choices, not traditional joint dimensions or engineering rules. A 1 mm third-member guard was also a geometric choice. The findings below show why these details cannot be promoted to a cutting schedule. The hip/ring bearing-datum conflict remains unresolved.

The recovered original 864 × 631 reference image has been inspected. It is an overview of the building, not a dimensioned corner-joint detail, and supplies no authenticated wing or concealed-joint detail. The source image does not validate the generated keys or resolve the modeled roof-bearing datum.

The viewer preserves all 104 timber IDs and 208 A/B references. Its optional edge measurements distinguish inside material angles from acute face-plane angles. Amber recess volumes show removed stock, including space occupied by mating timber; magenta shows the unfilled subset within original stock. Exterior trimming is not an internal defect. These inspection aids do not change the solids or the review’s construction limits.

2 · Structural geometry findings

B0 has only 233.75 mm² of wood at one measured internal section—0.755% of its original section. Its fitted extent begins 31.56 mm before the nominal axis start; the reported station is 70.93 mm after that start. This is an internal narrow section, not simply the pointed tip of a miter.

Every secondary rafter has a measured joint section near 16% of original stock area. For the nominal 2 × 6 inch stock, the conceptual key envelope is only 20.32 × 60.96 mm (0.8 × 2.4 inches). The brace and tie connections also remove substantial cross-sectional area.

Other entries require the original stock section to be at least 95% of its full nominal area. Area fractions are not strength or capacity ratios. Grain direction, bearing, bending, shear, tension, splitting and load transfer need separate analysis.

Connection force transfer is unresolved at the perimeter post/ring/hip/tie junctions, the cupola rings and the brace ends. A graph of touching parts does not establish a stable load path or uplift restraint. Roof diaphragm, foundations, anchors and cap-roof framing are also absent from the permanent model. The requested extended eaves change the load geometry and require wind/uplift and member checks. See S1 and S3.

No allowable roof load, snow capacity, member utilization ratio or pass/fail strength rating is issued. Site, timber and support inputs cannot be replaced by an assumed 600 ft² floor area.

3 · Machining and fit

Actual solid-wall witnesses measure about 1.000 mm in P0–P7, and about 2.000 mm locally in T0, R0 and C0. These are measured examples with solid material through the witness thickness; they are not a proof of each member’s absolute minimum thickness. A thin membrane can pass a watertight-mesh test and still be unsuitable for timber machining or load transfer.

There is no justified universal “add 1 mm everywhere” fix: that could reduce bearing, weaken remaining material or still leave a locked assembly. Real fabrication uses specified tolerances and movement detailing; a permanent gap-free condition at every wood surface is not established by exact CAD contact. See S2 and S4.

4 · Erection-path findings

The continuous motion audit tested 832 unique straight withdrawal paths across all 104 pieces, with the other pieces fixed. Tested direction families were vertical ±, member axis ±, horizontal radial ±, horizontal tangential ± and the current exploded offset, with duplicate directions removed. Every tested path has a positive-volume collision witness above 10⁻¹⁰ m³. A second run found collision in every path within the first 50 mm (1.97 inches). No piece could be selected as the first removal in this finite-direction disassembly search.

The test used continuous separating-axis intervals for convex cells, not only sampled pictures. Candidate collisions were independently checked by clipping the actual solids. Translation distance was 40 m, greater than twice the model bounding-box diagonal. This blocks a reverse assembly sequence using those single-piece straight paths; it does not prove that all possible rotated, staged or group assembly methods are impossible.

The current simultaneous exploded animation is also not a physical assembly path. J0-0 and B0 overlap by approximately 1,650 mm³ at 0.100% of that animation. The exploded slider remains a display tool.

The temporary black center assembly and gray shore remain temporary elements as requested. This audit gives no instruction to remove them at any particular stage. Removal needs a stage-specific load/stability check and completed permanent load paths. Temporary bracing and shoring must maintain stability during erection; a collision-free motion alone would not establish that (S2).

What the raising plan must resolve

  1. Choose and detail joints with a demonstrable insertion direction, including closure of each octagonal ring.
  2. Define allowed subassemblies and lifting points, then rerun continuous collision checks for those groups and motions.
  3. Analyze every partially completed frame, temporary support and lift case, including unbalanced loads and wind.
  4. Document support reactions, temporary bracing, survey/fit checks and criteria for removing each temporary element.

These are design requirements, not a field erection sequence. No safe lifting order or temporary-support capacity has been verified.
